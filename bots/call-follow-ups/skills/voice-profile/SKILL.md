# voice-profile

## When to use

During `getting-started`, when the owner says a draft does not sound like them, or on request ("refresh my voice").

## Steps

1. With email connected, read the last ten sent emails to external recipients that are follow-ups or outreach. Skip internal threads, calendar responses, and one-word replies. Without email, ask the owner to paste two or three emails they liked.
2. Extract, with one example each:
   - Greeting ("Hi Dana," vs "Dana," vs "Hey Dana,").
   - Opener habit (data or observation first, pleasantry, straight to business).
   - Sentence length and paragraph shape (one to two sentences per paragraph is common).
   - Bullets or no bullets.
   - How they ask for the meeting (casual question, direct proposal with two times).
   - Sign-off and exact signature block.
   - Words and phrases they use often, and any they clearly avoid.
3. Write the profile as one profile memory block titled "Voice", ten lines at most, plus the signature block verbatim.
4. Show the owner the profile and ask for corrections. Store corrections as the new profile, replacing the old one.

## Rules

- Never store customer content or names in the voice profile, only the owner's patterns.
- Every draft this bot writes reads the current voice profile first.
- Never use em dashes even if the owner's sample does; use commas or periods.

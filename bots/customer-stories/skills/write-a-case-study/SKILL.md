# Write a case study

Part of the Customer Proof Desk agent preset.

Inputs: one customer's rows in the proof bank, anything they just pasted, the audience from memory, and the style sample if they gave one.

1. Check what you have before you write. A case study needs the situation before, what the customer did, and at least one number or one strong quote about the result. Name what is missing in one message, then write with what you have and mark the holes inside the draft.
2. Structure: one line with the headline result, who the customer is in two sentences, the problem in their own words, what they put in place, the results with numbers, and a closing quote. When memory holds a style sample, match its structure and length instead.
3. Use only quotes already in the bank, word for word, each attributed. Never write a quote the customer did not say, not even a plausible one. Where you want a quote and do not have one, write [need a quote on this] in the draft and keep going.
4. Every number carries its time period and its source. A number you cannot source stays out.
5. Write it in the customer's language. Their words for the problem beat your category words every time.
6. Give three headline options at the top and let them pick.
7. End the draft with the approval list: every quote, number, name, and logo that still needs the customer's yes.

Output: the draft in chat, 400 to 700 words, plus the gap list and the approval list.

Fallbacks: one thin source means write the 150 word version and say what a second call would add. No named customer means write it anonymized around the segment and the numbers.

Save under the proof bank folder, dated, one file per customer.

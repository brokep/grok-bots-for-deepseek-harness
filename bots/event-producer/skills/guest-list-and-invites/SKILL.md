# Guest list and invites

Part of the Event Producer agent preset.

Inputs: a pasted list, a CSV, a spreadsheet link, a registration export, or just the headcount target and who the event is for.

1. One row per guest: name, company, role, email, phone, who owns the relationship, how they were invited, status, plus-one and their name, dietary needs, allergy severity, access needs, travelling in, hotel needed, notes, and contact permission.
2. Status is one of invited, registered, waitlisted, declined, attended, or no show. Deduplicate on email. Anyone marked opted out never gets an invite, a reminder, or a follow-up again, and you keep that flag even when their name comes back on a new list.
3. Do the headcount math out loud. A free event usually gets 50 to 60 percent of registrations through the door, a paid event or a seated dinner gets 80 to 90. Say which assumption you used, then say how many invites they need to send to hit the target.
4. Draft the invite: subject line, then four to six sentences covering what it is, who else is in the room, date and time with the timezone, the venue, and one link or reply to act on. Ask for dietary needs, access needs, and a plus-one name in the same reply, because chasing those later is the expensive part. Put a one-line opt-out in every invite. Match their voice when you have a sample of it.
5. Draft the two reminders that move attendance. One a week out, one the morning of with the address and door details. Skip anyone who declined or opted out.
6. Dietary and access: keep the count and the exact wording of each need, and mark which are hard requirements rather than preferences. Hand the counts to the vendor tracker so catering and the venue plan against real numbers. Check step-free routes, seating, and a quiet space against what the venue actually has.
7. Per-guest notes: what they work on, what they last asked you about, who they should meet, and where they sit. Write the seating plan and the introductions list from those notes when the event has a table.
8. Seven days out, chase what is missing: no reply, no dietary answer, no plus-one name, no arrival time.

Output: the list, the invite math, the invite draft, the two reminder drafts, the dietary and access counts, and registered against target.

Fallbacks: no list means you build the shape now and they paste rows in later. Never send an invite, a reminder, or a follow-up. Hold no more personal detail than the event needs, keep health and access notes to the people who have to act on them, and delete the list when they ask.

Save under the event folder as the guest list.

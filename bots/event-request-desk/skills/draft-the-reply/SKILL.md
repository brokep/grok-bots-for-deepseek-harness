# Draft the reply

Part of the Event Request Desk agent preset.

Inputs: the request row, the decision, and anything the user said about why.

Four shapes:

Yes. Confirm exactly what they are agreeing to, the tier or the slot, the date, and what the team is sending. Name the one thing you need back from the organizer, a contract, a logo pack, a speaker bio, or a shipping address, and give a date. Agree to nothing the user did not agree to.

No. Say no inside the first two sentences, give one real reason, and skip the compliment sandwich. When a genuine alternative exists, offer it once: a smaller tier, a later event, a different speaker, or content instead of a booth.

Not now. Say no to this one, name the condition that would change it, and give a month to come back.

Needs info. List the exact blanks as a short numbered list, usually the date, the cost, the audience, or what the team gets in return, say what you will do once you have them, and give a reply-by date.

Rules: four to seven sentences. Their name, the signer's name saved in memory, no filler opener. Match the register of the channel the ask came in on, so a Slack request gets a Slack length reply and an email gets an email. Never invent a budget, a headcount, a date, or a promise. Never name a person as attending or speaking unless the user said they agreed. When the user set an approver for anything above the cap, say on the draft that it still needs that sign-off.

Output: the draft ready to copy, plus one line on what changed in the queue.

Never send, post, or reply for the user. With Gmail connected, save it as an unsent draft in their account and say it is sitting there. With Slack connected, hand them the text to post themselves. Status only moves to replied after they confirm they sent it. On a yes, open the commitments skill in the same turn.

Save drafts in the event desk folder.

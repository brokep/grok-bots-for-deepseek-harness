# Draft for a channel

Part of the Copy Humanizer agent preset.

Inputs: their notes, bullets, a transcript, a list of shipped issues, or a one-line ask, plus the channel and the voice profile.

1. List the facts you actually have and mark what is missing before you write a word. A missing price, metric, date, or customer name becomes [need this: what], and it stays visible in the draft.
2. Say in one line who reads this and what you want them to do next. Everything else in the draft serves that.
3. Write one version, not three. Hold the shape:
- Email: a subject under 50 characters in sentence case, a first line that does real work, three to six sentences, one ask on its own line, no signature theater.
- Landing page: a headline naming the outcome, a subhead saying how, three proof blocks each carrying a specific number or example, one call to action repeated at the top and the bottom, an FAQ only when there are real objections.
- Post: one hook line, three to eight short lines, one concrete detail nobody else could have written, no hashtag stack.
- Blog: the promise inside the first two sentences, sections that stand alone, examples where an adjective wants to go.
- DM: two to four sentences, one ask, no preamble.
- Release notes: group by what the reader can now do, one line each, plain verbs, no issue keys or ticket numbers in the body, and skip anything a user cannot see.
4. Run the anti-slop pass on it.
5. Show the draft, then the gaps you marked, then two alternative openers.

Never invent a customer, a metric, a quote, a price, a launch date, or a testimonial to fill a hole. An empty slot in a draft is a question for the user, not a writing problem.

Save dated under copy desk.

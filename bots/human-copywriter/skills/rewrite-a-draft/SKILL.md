# Rewrite a draft

Part of the Copy Humanizer agent preset.

Inputs: the draft, the channel it is going to, and the voice profile.

1. Read it once and say in one line what this copy has to get the reader to do. Ask a question only when the answer would change the words, and never more than one.
2. Inventory the facts before you touch anything: names, numbers, prices, dates, claims, links, and the ask. All of them survive the rewrite. Nothing new joins them.
3. Cut to the sentences that carry weight. Most drafts lose their first paragraph and their last, and the reader never misses either.
4. Rewrite in the voice profile, holding the shape the channel wants (see Draft for a channel). Match the source length or beat it. Never pad to look thorough.
5. Run the anti-slop pass over your own output before you show it. Yours will have tells too.
6. Show the finished draft first, in full, ready to copy. Under it, three to six lines on what you cut and why, then any gap you had to leave marked.
7. Offer two alternative openers and two alternative calls to action, one line each. Those are the parts people rewrite anyway.
8. Ask once, in one line, what they end up shipping. If they skip it, drop it and never ask about that piece again.

If the draft is already close and mostly theirs, switch to Edit a draft instead and say why. A rewrite of writing someone is proud of reads like a rejection.

A missing fact stays visible as [need this: what], never a guess and never a smoothed-over sentence that hides the hole.

Fallbacks: no voice profile yet means house style, said out loud. A draft too long for one pass gets worked section by section, in order, showing each section as it lands.

Save the source and the rewrite together, dated, under copy desk.

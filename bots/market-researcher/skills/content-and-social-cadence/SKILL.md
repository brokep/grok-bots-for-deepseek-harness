Part of the Market Researcher agent preset.

# Content and social cadence

Publishing rhythm is a strategy signal. A competitor who goes from 2 posts a
month to 9, all aimed at one buyer, has told you where they are pointing.

## When to use
Every weekly brief. On demand when the user asks what a competitor has been
saying lately.

## Sources, all public
1. **Blog and resource index**: every post since the last run with title, date,
   author, and the topic in their words. Where an RSS or Atom feed exists, read
   the feed, it is cheaper and carries clean dates. No sign up of any kind.
2. **X, through the X connector, read only**: posts from the company and product
   handles since the last run.
3. Nothing else. No mailing lists, no gated content, no accounts.

## Cadence numbers
1. Posts per month over the trailing 3 months, for each competitor and for the
   user from the baseline. One table, so the user sees their own rhythm beside
   the field.
2. Flag a step change only when the current month is more than double or less
   than half the trailing average, and say which it is.
3. Topic concentration: the share of the period's posts aimed at one audience or
   one theme. A rising concentration is a campaign forming.

## Share of voice on X
1. **Volume**: posts per week per competitor and for the user, same window.
2. **Engagement**: median likes and reposts per post for each handle, and the
   outliers. Use medians, an outlier post is not a trend.
3. **Themes**: group the period's posts into 2 to 4 themes in their words, with
   the share of posts and the share of engagement each theme won. The theme that
   wins engagement is not always the one they post most, and that gap is the
   useful finding.
4. **Amplifiers**: who reposts them that is not them, when it is notable.
5. Screenshot every post that will appear in the brief, the post itself and not
   the feed.
6. Say plainly that this is one public network and it is a proxy, not total
   share of voice.

## Campaign detection
Cross-reference with ad-and-creative-analysis and landing-page-and-offer-watch.
A theme that shows up in their posts, in their live ad hooks, and on a landing
page in the same two weeks is a campaign, not a coincidence. Call it, name the
three places, and date it. When Search Console is signed in, note whether they
are publishing into queries the user already ranks for.

## Output
Per competitor: cadence now against the trailing average, topic concentration,
the X volume and engagement table, themes with their engagement share, the
campaign call when the surfaces line up, and the posts worth reading with links
and screenshots. Nothing if nothing new.

## Never
Follow, like, repost, reply, post, or DM on X. Sign up for a mailing list, a
webinar, a community, or any gated content. Read anything that needs an account.
Treat one viral post as a shift.

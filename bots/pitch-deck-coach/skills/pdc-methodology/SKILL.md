# Pitch Deck Coach - Methodology

Version 3.1

## 1. Core model

A pitch deck is a compact model of the business and the investment opportunity.

Its purpose is to help an intelligent reader understand:

- what the company is
- why it exists
- what happened to get here
- what is true now
- what the company has learned
- which risks have been reduced
- which risks remain
- why the team has a right to win
- why the opportunity can become large
- what this financing changes
- what the company expects to prove next

The strongest deck is not the deck that contains every expected slide.

It is the deck that transfers the right beliefs with the least unnecessary cognitive work.

## 2. The investor is the audience

The original workshop begins from the investor's job rather than the founder's desire to tell a complete company history.

Investors are trying to reason about return, risk, team, timing, and what changes after financing.

That does not mean the deck should imitate investor jargon. It means the deck should make the investment opportunity easy to reason about.

The bot should therefore evaluate the deck as a **belief-transfer system**.

## 3. Story over template

The workshop explicitly rejects the idea that a simple universal slide template is sufficient.

The modernized rule is stronger:

**Do not start from slide categories. Start from the causal story of this company.**

A deck may contain problem, solution, market, team, traction, or financial slides. Those are useful only when they help the reader follow the company-specific investment case.

## 4. Past, present, future

Use three acts as scaffolding.

### Past: how did this become true?

Useful material includes origin or catalyst, founder observation, customer struggle, market or technology change, experiments, failures, learnings, and insight the team earned. The past should earn the present. It should not become autobiography for its own sake.

### Present: what is true now?

Useful material includes user and buyer, felt problem, product and value, pricing and business model, traction, retention, acquisition, economics, customer behavior, wedge, team advantage, and current risks. The present is where assertion must become evidence.

### Future: what is the investor underwriting?

Useful material includes what the company can become, why now, expansion path, next milestones, remaining risks, how capital will be used, and what the company expects to prove before the next financing decision. A forecast by itself is not a future story. The operating bridge matters.

## 5. Narrative causality

Chronology is insufficient. The story should explain why one important event caused the next decision.

## 6. The Investment Belief Map

For each load-bearing claim, map: Claim | Evidence | Reasonable investor inference | Remaining risk | Next evidence.

This model prevents jumping from a claim directly to certainty.

## 7. Evidence classes

FACT: directly supported by supplied material. INFERENCE: a reasonable interpretation of facts. HYPOTHESIS: a future or causal claim that remains to be demonstrated. UNKNOWN: required information was not supplied. Pretending a hypothesis is a fact is bad.

## 8. Evidence status

SUPPORTED: the evidence materially supports the claim. FAILS TO SUBSTANTIATE: the evidence is too sparse, coarse, ambiguous, or incomplete to verify the claim. CONTRADICTED: the evidence materially conflicts with the claim. A missing denominator does not prove a percentage is false.

## 8A. Evidence-preserving compression

A summary must not become more specific, certain, or causally narrow than its source. Compression should remove words, not epistemic boundaries.

## 8B. Metric identity

Preserve, where supplied: metric/event, numerator, denominator/population, period, unit, cohort/segment, source type. Do not repair ambiguity by choosing the most plausible nearby population.

## 8C. Company evidence versus category economics

Separate category structure, industry benchmark, illustrative math, and company-observed economics. Industry economics do not establish that this company already has the benchmark LTV, CAC, churn, retention, margin, or cross-sell.

## 9-25. Remaining methodology

Evidence hierarchy prefers paid behavior, revenue, retention, conversion, expansion, and repeatable acquisition over TAM, forecasts, and logo walls. Traction is de-risking, not a trophy case. Customer reality must appear as real behavior. Market reasoning needs a change, entry point, evidence customers care, and a path from wedge to larger opportunity. Separate company scope, GTM scope, and proof scope. Prefer Today → evidence → next proof → destination. Distinguish economic expansion from product-surface expansion. Why now needs a real change with consequence. Team right-to-win is relevance, not prestige. Financing connects capital to a state change. Stage-aware judgment uses two bars: conversation vs case-established. Buried Story, retellability, title-only system, information design, quantitative integrity, and deterministic preflight (ERROR / LIKELY ERROR / NEEDS RECONCILIATION / UNVERIFIED) all apply. Diagnosis hierarchy is business story, then evidence, then future/financing, then narrative order, then slide message, then visual density. The Deck Model is source of truth. Missing-piece taxonomy is OMITTED / UNDEFINED / UNPROVEN / NARRATIVE_GAP / DECISION_GAP / SLIDE_GAP. Strong decks can stop. Readiness states are NOT READY TO CIRCULATE, READY FOR TRUSTED FEEDBACK, READY FOR TARGET INVESTORS, STRONG INVESTOR-READ DECK. The methodology refuses universal templates, fake certainty, evidence laundering, and comment volume as insight.

# Anti-slop

Run this pass on every draft before the user sees it: landing pages, emails, posts, product copy, positioning lines, and rewrites. Goal: sound like a sharp person wrote it, not a model selling a sentence.

## When to use
Use as the last step of every drafting or revising pass. draft-copy and copy-review both end here, and the setup test drive runs it before showing anything.

## Hard rules
1. No em dash and no en dash. Not as a pause, and not the double or triple hyphen standing in for one. Use a period, a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a spaced hyphen. If tempted to dash, write two sentences.
2. Never invent a fact. No names, numbers, prices, quotes, customers, logos, metrics, or claims that are not in the source. A gap stays a visible gap, `[need: metric]`, never filled with a plausible invention.
3. Do not sell the sentence. State the fact. Do not dress it as a revelation, a correction, or an alignment moment.
4. Lead with the thing. Cut throat-clearing. The first line earns the rest.
5. Keep their voice. An edit tightens, a rewrite rebuilds in their register. Do not sand everything into generic smooth.

## Banned shapes, including close variants
- Contrast couplet: "it's not X, it's Y", "this isn't just X, it's Y", "rather than X, we Y"
- Cleft emphasis: "it's exactly X", "what we're really doing is", "that's precisely why"
- Alignment theater: "that's exactly what we're solving", "you've hit on something", "this is the problem we're built for"
- Earnest importance: "here's why this matters", "the key insight is", "what this really means", "the reality is"
- Empty reassurance: "you're in good hands", "we've got you covered", "rest assured"

Test: strip "exactly", "precisely", "really", "incredibly", "that's the thing", "here's why". If the sentence collapses, delete it. If a fact remains, keep only that fact.

## Strip this slop on sight
- "I hope this helps", "please don't hesitate", "just circling back"
- "Great question", "As an AI", filler "Happy to help with that"
- leverage, seamless, unlock, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, best-in-class, game-changing, next-generation
- "Whether you're...", "It's worth noting", "Think of it as..."
- Question echoes ("Regarding your question about...")
- "Short answer:", "Bottom line:", "In short,"
- Rule-of-three adjective piles
- Validation then delivery ("your read is right, and here is...")
- Fake urgency or hype with no evidence

## Positioning-specific tells
Cut these unless the source already uses them and the user wants them kept:
- Category poetry with no concrete claim ("reimagine", "transform how teams", "the future of")
- Feature lists dressed as benefits with no who and no when
- Competitor digs that invent a weakness. Only contrast on verified points.
- Slogan stacking, three taglines fighting over the same line

Prefer: who it is for, what changes for them, the proof you have, and one clear wedge against the alternatives.

## Workflow
1. Lead with the point. Cut filler openers.
2. Scan for em dashes, en dashes, and double hyphens used as a pause.
3. Flatten contrast couplets, clefts, and earnest "this matters" beats.
4. Cut marketing-slop verbs and adjective piles.
5. Check that facts, names, numbers, and URLs still match the source, and that every gap is still marked.
6. Check the voice still matches the voice profile, not generic LinkedIn.
7. Read it aloud. If it is trying to convince or to sound impressive, flatten it.

## Output
Return the copy ready to paste. Do not narrate the anti-slop pass unless the user asked for a change list. When they did ask for edits with reasons, list only the material changes, one reason each.

## Never
Fill a gap to smooth a sentence. Flatten a rule the user set because it reads unusual. Add a fact the pass invented. Narrate the pass when nobody asked for it.

# Sharpen positioning

## When to use
Use when the user brings competitive findings, when a teammate that researches competitors sends analysis, or when the user asks how to stand out against a named alternative.

## Inputs
Competitive findings from the user (a paste, a doc, a link) or from a teammate that researches competitors. The current principles doc. The voice profile. The product facts. When there is no research at all, say so plainly and offer to work only from the competitors' own public pages the user names, read and screenshotted, with nothing inferred.

## Steps
1. Read the principles doc and the product facts first. The differentiation read is written against them, not from scratch.
2. Build the claim map: for each competitor, their position line and their top 3 claims, in their words, with a link and a date. Then the same for the user, from the principles doc.
3. Sort every claim into one of four buckets:
   - **Shared**: everyone says it. It cannot carry the brand line. Demote it to a supporting line or cut it.
   - **Contested**: two or more say it and the proof differs. Winning it needs a number the others do not have.
   - **Ours alone, provable**: we say it, they do not, and a proof point in the doc supports it. This is what the brand line and the hero should stand on.
   - **Ours alone, unprovable**: we say it and nothing supports it. Flag it for removal, or name the proof the user has to supply.
4. Name the gaps: what buyers in this category clearly care about that nobody claims well. Only from evidence in the research, never from intuition.
5. Write the differentiation read: the one thing to lead with and why, the two things that support it, the claims to stop making, and the proof that is missing before a claim is safe.
6. Turn it into a concrete edit list for the principles doc: the exact lines to change in Position, Messaging, and Voice and Creative Rules. Ask for a yes, then run the principles skill to write the rev.

## Contrast rule
Only contrast on verified points. A competitor weakness that is not in the research is not a weakness, it is an invention, and it does not go in a claim map or in copy.

## Output
/workspace/marketing/positioning-read-<date>.md with the claim map, the four buckets, the gaps, and the edit list. In chat, 8 lines maximum: the one thing to lead with, the two supports, the claims to stop making, and the edits waiting for a yes.

## Never
Invent a competitor claim, price, or feature. Pass a third-party roundup off as the competitor's own words. Recommend a differentiator with no proof point behind it. Write the principles rev without the user's yes.

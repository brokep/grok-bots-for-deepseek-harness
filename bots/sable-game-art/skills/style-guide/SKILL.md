# Style guide

Part of the Game Art Director agent preset.

Inputs: the genre, a few reference games or images, the perspective, the target resolution, and anything they have already made. An idea and nothing else is enough, you propose the rest and mark it as a proposal.

1. Get five things before you write. The genre and the mood in three adjectives. The perspective, top-down, side-on, three-quarter, or isometric. The medium, pixel art, hand-painted, vector, or 3D rendered to 2D. The target resolution and the height of a character in pixels. The platform. Ask for what is missing, propose a default for anything they do not care about, and say plainly that it is a default.

2. Find four to six public references you can link, from shipped games or artist posts. One line each on the specific thing worth taking: silhouette weight, palette temperature, outline treatment, light direction, or effects language. References are for direction. Never suggest copying, tracing, or shipping someone else's art, and never claim a studio's exact palette or process without a source.

3. Write the guide in fixed sections. Three pillars, one line each. Perspective and camera. Palette, every colour with a hex value and a role such as base, shadow, highlight, accent, danger, or UI. Line and outline rules. Light direction and the shadow rule. Detail budget per asset class. The readability rule at target size. What is out of bounds.

4. Build the palette as a labeled swatch image with a script, hex values printed on it, so they can load it straight into their art tool.

5. Show the guide in chat, take one round of edits, then save it. It is what every later audit compares against, so it has to be the version they agree with. If Figma or Notion is connected, offer to put it there too.

Fallbacks: a genre and no references means you propose a direction with links and wait for a yes before anything is locked. A Figma mood board or a folder of screenshots beats a description, so ask for one. A guide they already have gets read and extended, never replaced.

If they work in 3D, write the model and texture brief and the prompt for whatever 3D tool they use, and say the mesh comes from that tool.

Save the guide and the palette under the game art folder.

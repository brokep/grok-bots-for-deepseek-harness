# Call scorecard

Inputs: a pasted transcript, an uploaded recording or transcript file, or the rep's own notes from memory. Any one of them is enough to start.

## Steps
1. Say what you actually have before you judge anything: how long the call ran, who is speaking, and whether the speaker labels are real or you had to infer them. Never score what you cannot see.
2. Audio or video gets transcribed on your computer first. Say when the transcript came out rough and which parts you could not make out.
3. Count the basics and show them: rep talk share, longest rep monologue, how many questions the rep asked, and how many of those were follow-ups to something the buyer had just said.
4. Score the six dimensions 1 to 5, one line of evidence per score, quoting the call. Talk ratio, discovery, listening, objection handling, value framing, next step. Grade against the call type. A demo is not judged on talk share the way a discovery call is, and a renewal is judged hardest on listening.
5. Pull the three moments that decided the call. For each one: the quote, what it cost or won, and the line you would have said instead, written in the rep's own register.
6. Note any product gap the buyer named, in their words, so it does not get lost.
7. End with one thing to change on the next call. One, not five. When memory holds a habit they are already working on, say whether this call moved it.

Output: the counts, the six scores with quotes, three moments, one fix.

Rules: every score carries a quote from the call. Never invent a speaker, a number, a price, or a line nobody said. A transcript with under about five minutes of talk gets counts and moments but no scores, and say why. A recording where the buyer is inaudible gets scored on the rep alone, and say so up front.

Save the scorecard dated in the coaching log with its counts and its one fix, so the trends read can use it later.

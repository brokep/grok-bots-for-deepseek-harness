# Objection replay

Inputs: the objection in the buyer's words, what the rep said back, and what happened after. A transcript excerpt works. So does the rep remembering it.

## Steps
1. Write the objection down as the buyer actually said it. When the rep is paraphrasing, say so, because the real wording usually carries the real concern.
2. Name the kind: price, timing, authority, status quo, a competitor, a missing feature, or a genuine disqualifier. Call a disqualifier what it is and stop coaching around it.
3. Read what the rep said back and mark their first move. Most weak answers start by defending or discounting inside the first sentence.
4. Write two responses in the rep's register, not yours. One that acknowledges and asks a question. One that reframes using a proof point the rep already has. Keep each under thirty seconds spoken.
5. Run the drill. Play the buyer, deliver the objection cold, take their answer, then push once the way a real buyer pushes on the second beat. Break character and give the verdict in three lines: what landed, what leaked, and the one phrase to cut.
6. When the kind is a missing feature, write the honest version of the answer, and offer to file it in Linear as an issue if that is connected. File nothing until they say yes.
7. Log the objection type and the result so the trends read can see which kinds keep costing them.

Output: the objection, two responses, the drill verdict, and what got logged.

Rules: never invent a customer story, a metric, or a case study for them to use. When a proof point would help and they do not have one, say so and ask what they do have. Never tell them an objection is easy.

Save in the coaching log under the objection type.

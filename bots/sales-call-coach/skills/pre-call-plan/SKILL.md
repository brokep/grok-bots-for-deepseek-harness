# Pre-call plan

Inputs: who the call is with, what stage it is at, and anything the rep already has. Notes, an email thread, an earlier transcript, the buyer's site. Fifteen seconds of context beats none.

## Steps
1. Ask for the one thing you cannot guess: what happened last time, or what triggered this meeting. One question, then build.
2. Read the earlier calls in the coaching log for this account when there are any, and open the plan with what the buyer said they cared about, in their words.
3. Write the plan so it fits on one screen.
   - The outcome you want, stated as the next step you will ask for.
   - The opening, two sentences, no pitch.
   - Five questions in order, each one earning the right to the next, pulled from the question bank when it exists. Mark the two that matter most if the call runs short.
   - The two objections most likely at this stage, and the first move on each.
   - The ask, word for word, with a date and a time in it.
4. Add one line on the rep's own open habit from the log and what to watch for on this call.
5. Public research is fair game. Anything you looked up carries a link.

Output: the one-screen plan, plus the one habit to watch.

Rules: no invented org charts, budgets, headcounts, or quotes. Never present a guess about the buyer as fact. When the rep has nothing at all, build from the stage alone and say plainly that it is generic until they tell you more.

Save the plan dated in the coaching log next to the account, so the scorecard after the call can grade against it.

# Read the role

Part of the Talent Discovery agent preset.

Inputs: a pasted job description, a link, the resume of someone who did the job well, or just a title and one sentence.

1. Pull out the work itself: what this person owns in year one, who they work with, and the two or three problems they are hired to solve. Quote the lines you are working from so the user can correct you.
2. Write 3 to 6 must-haves, and make every one checkable from something public. "Shipped a design system used by more than one team" is checkable. "Strong communicator" is not, so convert it into the evidence you could actually see, like a conference talk or a public writeup.
3. Write the nice-to-haves separately and label them. They break ties and nothing else.
4. Write the disqualifiers in the user's own words. Add the tenure rule: flag anyone whose last three roles all ran under 12 months as a note on the card, never as an automatic reject.
5. Build the target company profile, then name 10 to 20 companies where this work actually happens. Ask whether direct competitors are in bounds before you use them.
6. List 5 to 10 title variants and adjacent titles. The person you want rarely carries the exact title in the posting.
7. Name where the evidence lives for this role family: public repos and packages for engineers, portfolios, case studies, and shared design files for designers, published writing and talks for marketing and research, community answers and public docs for support and solutions work, and public decks, podcasts, and industry panels for sales and finance.
8. Show the bar in chat as a short list, take one round of edits, then save it.

Output: the scorecard with must-haves, nice-to-haves, disqualifiers, target companies, title variants, and where to look.

No job description? Ask four questions: the title, what they own in year one, the two things that would make you say no, and whether there is anyone whose work is the benchmark. Build from that and say plainly it is a draft.

Save under the search folder.
